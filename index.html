Albert Einstein was a theoretical physicist and a scientist, known for his theory of relativity and E = mc² energy equals mass times the speed of light. For his first job his friend found him a position as a clerk in the Swiss Federal Patent Office in Bern. Albert Einstein liked playing the violin and studying math, science, and physics. He also liked reading, and wanted to write his own scientific papers. In 1916, Einstein's paper on the general theory of relativity explained that gravity isn't a mysterious pull, but a dip in space caused by heavy objects. It shows that planets curve spacetime, forcing stars and planets to orbit eachother. He started to work on quantum mechanics, but never finished his theory. Albert Einstein did lots of research important to us even today. For example, he explained the photoelectric effect and won a Nobel Prize for it. His research also led to TV's being invented. First, light from whatever is being filmed hit a light sensitive surface. Next, because of the photoelectric effect, the light released electrons from the surface. Then, the pattern of electrons matched the light and dark in the picture, which is why early TV's were only black and white. The pattern was then converted into an electric signal. Finally, the signal is sent to the TV to make the picture, and lots of these strung together makes a video. In 1930, Einstein traveled to the United States to lecture at the California Institute of Technology, but in 1933, the Nazi party took control of Germany and Einstein could never go back. So Einstein went to Belgium, then to England, and in September 1933 he moved to the U.S. to work at the Institute for Advanced Study. He became a U.S. citizen in October 1940. In 1939, he wrote a letter to President Franklin Roosevelt warning that the German scientists might be working on an atomic bomb. This led to the Manhattan Project, which created the first atomic bomb in the U.S. in 1945. In 1952, he was offered to become the president of Israel, but he declined.
